1 · Capacity: machines per station

The $1,250 contract pays in full only if a job finishes within 12 hours, and a job needs 9.3 hours of pure processing. That leaves under 3 hours of total queueing, so every station must run well below full load. Rule: add machines until each station is at or under about two-thirds utilization.

4 · End game: the last kit order (do this before Day 218)

After Day 218 you lose control, and leftover kits are worth nothing. Place one final order that covers demand to the end, then stop all ordering.

  1. Wait until no kit order is open (the Materials panel shows none outstanding), or enter the open amount above.
  2. Type the final quantity into Order Quantity, then raise Reorder Point above the kits on hand. The order fires at once.
  3. Check the order shows as placed, then set Order Quantity to 0. Ordering stops for good.
  4. Leave the machines and contract 3 in place. Retiring a machine returns only $10,000; the simulation shows the 5th stuffer earns about $29,000 more than that over the last 50 days, and retiring a tester or tuner wrecks lead times.

Playbook: what to do and when

  1. Day 50, first hour: download the data and enter the average orders per day above. Buy the machines from panel 1.
  2. Day 50: set Order Quantity, then Reorder Point, from panel 3.
  3. Days 50–53: watch the queues drain. When jobs in the system are ≤ about 6, switch to contract 3.
  4. Every day or two: check that lead times stay under 12 hours, utilization stays under about 70%, and kits never hit zero. If demand runs well above your estimate, re-enter it here and buy the extra machine the table shows.
  5. Days 205–217: place the final order from panel 4, then set Order Quantity to 0.
